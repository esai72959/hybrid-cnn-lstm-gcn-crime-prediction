Conclusion

Neither spatial nor temporal modeling alone captures the full complexity of crime occurrence patterns. The standalone CNN reached an R² score of {{ metrics.cnn.r2 }} ({{ metrics.cnn.accuracy }}% of variance explained) by identifying localized spatial hotspots, while the standalone LSTM reached {{ metrics.lstm.r2 }} ({{ metrics.lstm.accuracy }}%) by modeling sequential temporal dependencies.

The proposed Hybrid CNN-LSTM framework fuses spatial embeddings from the convolutional branch with temporal embeddings from the recurrent branch, correlating where crimes are likely to occur with when they are likely to occur. This joint representation raised the R² score to {{ metrics.hybrid.r2 }} ({{ metrics.hybrid.accuracy }}% of variance explained), with the lowest RMSE ({{ metrics.hybrid.rmse }}) and MAE ({{ metrics.hybrid.mae }}) of the three architectures - lower error values indicate closer agreement between predicted and actual crime counts. Note that R² here measures explained variance in a regression task and is not classification accuracy.
